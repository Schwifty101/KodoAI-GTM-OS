# LinkedIn DM Framework

The goal of LinkedIn outreach is **not to pitch your product**.

The goal is to **start conversations with qualified prospects by proving you understand their world.**

---

# Core Principles

Everything comes down to three things:

1. **Relevance** — Make the message about them.
2. **Specificity** — Prove you've done your homework.
3. **Consistency** — Follow a repeatable outreach system.

---

# Rule 1 — Earn the Right to DM

Don't rely on InMail.

Instead:

1. Send a personalized connection request.
2. Wait for acceptance.
3. Start the conversation via DM.

The connection acceptance is permission to start a conversation.

---

# Rule 2 — Use Voice Notes Strategically

Voice notes create trust because they:

- Feel human
- Show effort
- Build rapport faster than text

Use them for:

- Following up with non-responders
- Re-engaging cold leads
- High-priority prospects

Don't send voice notes to everyone.

---

# Rule 3 — Use Video for High-Value Prospects

Video is the strongest trust builder because it:

- Interrupts the normal pattern
- Shows personality
- Demonstrates effort

Reserve video for:

- High-priority prospects
- Warm leads that went cold
- Referral or event follow-ups

---

# Rule 4 — Have a System

Treat LinkedIn like a CRM.

Suggested pipeline:

- Connected
- DM Sent
- Responded
- Meeting Scheduled
- Nurture
- Closed Lost

Consistency beats random outreach.

---

# Rule 5 — Give Value Before You Pitch

Never start with your company.

Start with **their business.**

DM Formula:

## 1. Observation

Something specific you noticed.

> "Only two of your AEs posted on LinkedIn in the last month."

---

## 2. Implication

Why that matters.

> "That's likely costing qualified inbound opportunities."

---

## 3. Value

Offer something useful.

> "I built a framework that fixes this."

---

## 4. Low-Friction Ask

Ask for almost nothing.

Examples:

- Open to seeing it?
- Want me to send it over?
- Worth sharing?

---

# Rule 6 — Be Hyper-Specific

Generic personalization isn't personalization.

Bad:

> Congrats on the new role!

Good:

> Really enjoyed your post about reverse selling, especially your point about asking prospects what would disqualify you.

Good personalization comes from:

- Recent posts
- Promotions
- Company news
- Hiring
- Product launches
- Profile experience
- About section

Spend 30–60 seconds researching before messaging.

---

# Rule 7 — Ask One Question

Every additional question creates friction.

Bad:

- Are you using LinkedIn?
- How's outbound?
- What's your biggest challenge?
- When can we chat?

Good:

> Are you currently using LinkedIn for outbound?

Best question types:

- Yes / No
- Clarifying
- Permission-based

Avoid:

- Multiple questions
- Huge open-ended questions

One message.

One question.

---

# Rule 8 — Make It About Them

Count pronouns.

Too many:

- I
- We
- Our
- My

Not enough:

- You
- Your

Lead with:

- Their business
- Their goals
- Their problem
- Their opportunity

People care about themselves before they care about your solution.

---

# Rule 9 — Mirror Their Communication Style

Adapt to how they communicate.

Mirror:

- Response speed
- Message length
- Tone
- Formality
- Emoji usage
- Energy

People naturally trust people who communicate like they do.

---

# Rule 10 — End With a Clear Next Step

Never let conversations fade.

Always finish with a simple next action.

Examples:

- Want me to send the framework?
- Open to learning more?
- Thursday at 3 or Friday at 9?

Never drop a scheduling link immediately.

Suggest two times instead.

```text
Research
      ↓
Specific Observation
      ↓
Explain Why It Matters
      ↓
Offer Value
      ↓
One Simple Question
      ↓
Clear Next Step
```

# The Three Pillars

## 1. Relevance

Make every message about the prospect.

Not about you.

---

## 2. Specificity

Reference something real.

Examples:

- Recent post
- Promotion
- Company announcement
- Hiring
- Industry trend

Specificity proves effort.

---

## 3. Systems

Daily consistency wins.

Example cadence:

25 DMs/day
↓
125/week
↓
500/month
↓
6,000/year

Great outreach isn't about intensity.

It's about consistency.

# LLM Prompt

## Task

Rewrite the following LinkedIn outreach message using this framework.

### Step 1 — Research & Personalize

- Reference something real and specific.
- Use recent posts, company news, hiring, promotions, product launches, or profile details.
- Avoid generic compliments.

### Step 2 — Lead With an Observation

- Identify something specific about the prospect's business.
- Demonstrate that you've done your homework.

### Step 3 — Explain the Implication

- Explain why the observation matters.
- Highlight the cost, risk, or missed opportunity.

### Step 4 — Offer Value

- Share a useful insight, framework, resource, or idea.
- Don't pitch immediately.
- Give before asking.

### Step 5 — Ask One Low-Friction Question

Ask only one simple question.

Prefer:

- Yes/No
- Clarifying
- Permission-based

Examples:

- Open to seeing it?
- Worth sharing?
- Is this relevant?

### Step 6 — End With a Clear Next Step

Always suggest a concrete next action.

Examples:

- Thursday at 3 or Friday at 9?
- Want me to send the framework?
- Happy to share an example.

## Writing Style

- Conversational
- Short sentences
- Plain English
- Customer-centric
- Specific
- Helpful
- Non-pushy
- Confident
- Human
- No corporate jargon
- No hype
- No generic personalization
- Make the reader feel understood before presenting the solution.

## Output Format

Return:

1. Personalized Opening
2. Observation
3. Implication
4. Value Offer
5. Low-Friction Question
6. Final LinkedIn DM
