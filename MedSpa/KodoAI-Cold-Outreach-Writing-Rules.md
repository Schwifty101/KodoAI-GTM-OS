# KodoAI Cold Outreach Writing Rules

Final as of October 10, 2026. For the MedSpa project.

Status: finalized to Soban's decisions in this chat. These rules support the four updated governing files. They do not authorize sending an email without approval or override duplicate, DNC, evidence, platform or logging requirements.

## Decisions now governing this project

Keep the relevance-led fallback. Web research cannot reveal every internal project. When no current signal is observed, test priority through a sourced, observed handoff that meets the full relevance-led gate.

Soban does not have client examples to ground this campaign's demand assumptions. A KodoAI client, case study or prior buyer-confirmed project is not a prerequisite for cold outreach. Research can establish a specific reason to ask without establishing intent to buy.

Soban's team has worked with major enterprises, verified against Bluegile's published portfolio (bluegile.xyz/portfolio, read October 10, 2026). Touch 1 names a few of those clients as credibility and does not mention Bluegile. That shows who Soban's team is. It is not demand proof and not med-spa experience. KodoAI itself has no med-spa client examples, and no email may imply otherwise.

Use sourced evidence of other providers serving a workflow to choose market and offer opportunities to test. Treat this as market-level context. Keep the individual buyer's priority unconfirmed until the buyer supplies evidence.

Every email ends with exactly one question. Touch 3 replaces the former close-off with one partner-fit line and a question about the original objective.

## Touch 1: the five rules

Source: Soban's reference “5 Rules for Good Cold Emails,” adapted for KodoAI. These govern the order and length of Touch 1 only. Every evidence, claim and offer restriction elsewhere still applies. The approved client names and the case study behind each one are in `Med-Spa-Touch-1-Research-Master-Prompt.md`, section 8b.

| # | Rule | How it applies to a KodoAI Touch 1 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 to 8 sentences, max | Count the body only, not the subject, greeting or signature; the question counts. The Touch 1 limit of 45 to 70 words excluding subject (greeting and signature included) also applies and binds first, so write about five short sentences of roughly 12 words each. |
| 2 | Lead with who you are and why you're worth talking to | Open with “I'm Soban, founder of KodoAI.” and one sentence naming enterprise clients, such as “We've worked with Rolls-Royce, Saudi Aramco and Patagonia.” Do not mention Bluegile. The prospect's signal comes second. |
| 3 | Personalize. But stay on topic! | One sentence, two at most: the observed signal and the observed handoff. No compliment, rapport detail or other fact about the business. |
| 4 | Explain in 1-2 sentences how you think you can help them | “We build custom AI systems around [vector-specific outcome].” Add a second sentence beginning “I think we could...” only if the email stays within 70 words. No numbers, guarantees, savings or claims about unobserved performance. |
| 5 | Write like a founder, not an AE | First person, plain words, short sentences, contractions allowed. One question and no meeting or demo ask. Avoid “I wanted to reach out,” “I hope this finds you well,” “touch base,” “circle back,” “quick question,” “reach out,” “synergy,” “leverage,” “best-in-class,” “cutting-edge,” “game-changing,” “unlock,” “empower,” “seamless,” “turnkey,” “pain points” and “solutions” as a vague noun. |

Word budget for the whole email, excluding subject: greeting 1, introduction 5, credibility about 9, signal and handoff about 15 to 20, how we can help about 15, question about 15, signature 4. The relevance-led lane drops the signal and states the handoff only.

### Credibility line (named clients)

Touch 1 does not mention Bluegile (Soban's decision). Credibility is one sentence naming two or three enterprise clients, such as “We've worked with Rolls-Royce, Saudi Aramco and Patagonia.” Names come only from the approved list in the Touch 1 prompt, and each must appear in a case study on Bluegile's published portfolio. The email never cites that portfolio.

Never state or imply:

- a med-spa, aesthetics or healthcare client;
- that any named company uses, endorses or referred KodoAI. Say “we've worked with,” never “our customers,” “trusted by” or “clients include”;
- what the work was beyond what the case study says, or any result, saving, rating or client count;
- names that appear on bluegile.xyz only as logos (Patreon, Nutrabolt, Jazzcash), unless Soban confirms them in chat for that send;
- SOC2 or other compliance wording, or any pricing.

If a prospect asks, answer plainly that the work was done through Soban's parent company, Bluegile Technologies.

## Writing rules

| Rule | Apply it this way | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Start with the buyer's work | Name one booking or operating objective before choosing an AI capability. | RS06, RS09 |
| Keep both outreach lanes | Use a verified current signal when available; otherwise apply the observed-handoff relevance gate. | Soban's decision; governing prompts |
| Keep market evidence separate | Evidence that an offering works elsewhere can guide targeting, but does not prove this buyer's priority. | Soban's strategy; RS11 |
| Do not require your own case study | Qualify this prospect honestly and test its priority; do not invent customer results. | Soban's decision |
| Separate frustration from demand | An awkward journey does not establish an active buying project. | RS07 |
| Make the reason to respond specific | The internal Reason to Respond must explain why this business could care. | Governing guide; RS01 |
| Keep research relevant | Use observations that explain the chosen handoff; omit details included merely to display effort. | RS01 |
| Ask about inferred priorities | A signal or observed handoff justifies a question, not an assertion about an unconfirmed goal. | Touch 1 prompt; RS05 |
| Check existing options | Staff, native software and workarounds may already complete the exact task. | RS03, RS07; platform screens |
| Describe one capability | Keep KodoAI's capability tied to the selected vector and observed handoff. | RS06; approved templates |
| Earn interest honestly | Use true, relevant experience only. The named-client list above is the only experience Touch 1 may cite. Never invent credentials, clients, results or an advisor/research pretext. | RS02; project evidence rules |
| Remove defensive sales language | Avoid vague feedback requests, “not selling,” busy-person disclaimers and manufactured urgency. | RS01, RS06 |
| Keep Touch 2 narrow | Same thread, original objective, one question; no new material. | RS01 supports a bump; exact copy is KodoAI's |
| Make Touch 3 selective through fit | State readiness to prioritize the same objective as the partner criterion, then test it directly. | Soban's October 2 decision |
| Learn from actual responses | Use what buyers say and do to refine targeting; do not treat silence, compliments or interest as purchase intent. | RS05, RS08, RS10 |

Use US English, plain conversational language and no em dashes. Avoid generic compliments, fake familiarity, jargon and clinical judgment. Introduce Soban as founder, and use “We build custom AI systems...” for KodoAI's capability. Write like a founder, not an AE (rule 5 above).

Keep one question at the end. Use observed facts in prospect claims; inferred objectives belong in questions; unknowns stay out of claims. Keep the priority-first CTA. Touch 1 keeps 45 to 70 words and the five-rule sentence limit; Touch 2 and Touch 3 keep their word limits.

## Early-stage market selection

Soban calls his strategy the dam/flood approach: investigate what is already working, then test a relevant offering in that opportunity.

Rob's Waves, dams, and rivers discusses how demand can change and later entrants can find opportunity after an initial breakthrough. It is strategic context, not proof that any med-spa market is currently experiencing that pattern. [RS11]

Useful category evidence includes a sourced customer example, a documented implementation or a concrete workflow described by a buyer. Record attribution honestly: a vendor's stated result is a vendor claim unless independently verified. Do not invent KodoAI outcomes from another provider's example.

Category research is optional context for selecting an offer; it creates no new mandatory per-prospect field or gate. It does not make prospect-specific competitor research mandatory. Naming a competitor in an email still requires the existing recent-action conditional.

The prospect relevance case still needs an observed handoff, a business-specific reason to respond and platform screening. Existing options may already work. Neither a market example nor a public growth signal proves an individual buyer needs to switch.

## Current sequence and templates

| Touch | Timing | Copy constraints |
|---|---|---|
| Touch 1 | Day 0 | 45–70 words excluding subject, and 5 to 8 sentences in the body. Introduction and credibility → signal → handoff → how we can help → priority question; or introduction and credibility → handoff → how we can help → priority question when relevance-led. |
| Touch 2 | Day 3 | 10–35 words excluding greeting/signature. Same-thread bump using the original objective. |
| Touch 3 | Day 7 | 15–40 words excluding greeting/signature. Same-thread partner-fit line and one direct question about the same objective. |

There is no Touch 4. Replies, bounces, opt-outs, auto-replies and later manual messages stop the scheduled sequence for review.

### Touch 1, signal-led

```text
[Name],

I'm Soban, founder of KodoAI. We've worked with [two or three names from the approved client list].

[Company] [verified, time-bound signal]. [Specific customer or operational task] still [observed handoff stated without interpretation].

We build custom AI systems around [vector-specific outcome]. [Optional, only if the email stays within 70 words: I think we could [how that applies to the observed handoff].]

Is [specific objective connected to the signal] something you're actively working on this quarter?

Soban | Founder, KodoAI
```

### Touch 1, relevance-led

```text
[Name],

I'm Soban, founder of KodoAI. We've worked with [two or three names from the approved client list].

[Company]'s [observed handoff, stated as fact, no interpretation].

We build custom AI systems around [vector-specific outcome]. [Optional, only if the email stays within 70 words: I think we could [how that applies to the observed handoff].]

Is [objective tied to that handoff] something you're actively working on right now?

Soban | Founder, KodoAI
```

Use the vector-specific outcome table in the Touch 1 prompt. The relevance-led question has no soft opt-out.

### Touch 2

```text
[Name],

Is [objective tested in Touch 1] something you're working on right now, or is it not a priority?

Soban
```

### Touch 3

```text
[Name],

We're choosing med-spa partners who are ready to make [objective tested in Touch 1] a priority this quarter.

Is that where [Company] is right now?

Soban
```

The Touch 3 line describes selection by readiness to act. It does not imply an existing med-spa client base, a busy pipeline or a numerical cap. Do not claim limited quarterly availability, nearly filled slots or a deadline unless Soban confirms a real operating basis. “Select partners each quarter” is not a default availability claim.

Carry forward the original objective. Adjust its grammar or length without changing the priority being tested. Add no new prospect facts, research, proof points, performance claims, numbers, diagnosis, benefit argument, competitor comparison, trial, asset, meeting or call.

The Touch 1 relevance-led question has no soft opt-out. Touch 2 may use “or is it not a priority?” These are touch-specific rules.

### How to use the research

Keep a specific buyer's work and available options in view. Let real observations earn the question, preserve uncertainty honestly, and revise the playbook from actual responses. The current governing prompts control execution.
